They didn't work out easily. I had to adapt to entirely new corporate cultures, prove myself repeatedly in rooms where I didn't look like everyone else, and figure out how Australian business actually works vs. how it presents itself. I went from Sapient (2015, India) to navigating roles at Suncorp, Chubb Insurance, Trideca, and NAB — each one teaching me something different about delivering in complex, ambiguous environments.

Eventually, I became an Australian citizen. That wasn't just an admin milestone — it represented a genuine commitment to this country and its community. It means I have skin in the game here, not just a career.

Now I'm doing an EMBA at RMIT, specifically to build the strategic and governance layer that complements my technical depth. The goal isn't just to be good at AI — it's to lead it at an enterprise scale where the decisions actually matter.

## Leadership Philosophy
I am **purpose-driven, ethical, and accountable**. These aren't values I put on a slide — they're operating principles:

- **"Purpose before pace. Ethics before optics."** — My actual motto. I'd rather make a slower, defensible decision than a fast one that blows up later.
- **Psychological safety** — I create environments where people can say what's actually happening, not what they think you want to hear.
- **Decision logs and OKRs** — I use accountability mechanisms, not vibes. If we said we'd do something, it gets tracked.
- **Stakeholder alignment** — I'm obsessive about making sure everyone is pointed in the same direction before we accelerate. Misalignment at speed is just expensive chaos.
- **De-risking delivery** — I think about what can go wrong before I ship, not after. Risk registers, dependency maps, clear protocols.
